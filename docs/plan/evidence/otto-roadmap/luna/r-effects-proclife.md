## Durable-effects ownership conflict and verdict

The project plans point in different directions. EFFECTS says every external effect should have one identity in Herzchen’s kernel and one reconciliation procedure ([northstar.md](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/61c0e870-3601-52f0-903b-3607ce5e0548/portfolio/projects/effects/northstar.md:3)). PROCLIFE explicitly defers a general obligations ledger until there is another remote provider or multi-machine scheduling ([tasklist.md](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/61c0e870-3601-52f0-903b-3607ce5e0548/portfolio/projects/process-lifecycle/tasklist.md:10)). **Inference:** the underlying dispute is whether the kernel ledger owns durable effect identity/settlement broadly, or each subsystem owns its specific durable process and occurrence custody.

They need not be mutually exclusive if responsibilities are explicit: the effect ledger can own idempotency and receipts, while Proclife’s latches retain exact process identity and cleanup evidence. But EFFECTS’ “one ledger for every external effect” ambition exceeds its demonstrated coverage, while Proclife intentionally does not provide that general ledger.

**Verdict:** advance EFFECTS as the stronger foundation for hidden at-most-once tool behavior, subject to its uncovered-effect and acceptance gaps. Treat PROCLIFE as complementary process-custody work; it does not satisfy the target’s liveness-monitor or general receipts goals by itself. Neither branch alone delivers the full target.
